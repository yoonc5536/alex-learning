⭐ Star Specimens

These 8 got the most attention on our visit. Tap a card's photo to zoom, and hit ★ Collect to add your favorites to your treasure tray at the bottom!

★ Calcite FIZZES!

The mineral that fizzes like soda
What: Calcium carbonate — the stuff of limestone and marble.
Party trick: Drop vinegar on it and it fizzes! It also breaks into slanted little boxes.
Hardness: 3 — a copper penny can scratch it. This bin: green calcite, $3.00 a piece!
📚 Science class link: mineral ID tests — hardness, cleavage, and the acid test beat color every time.

★ Silicified Wood TREE → STONE

A real tree that turned into rock
What: Wood turned to quartz, cell by cell, over millions of years.
How: Buried fast (no rotting!) + silica-rich water = every ring and knot saved in stone.
Look: This polished slice — the wood's structure is still there, now solid agate-like stone!
Note: This photo is from the web (our show photo turned out to be dinosaur bone!).
📷 Web photo: Michael Gäbler via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0) — not from Rocktoberfest.
📚 Science class link: fossil formation — petrification. Fossils aren't just bones!

★ Amber BUG TRAP!

Sticky tree sap turned to golden time capsule
What: Hardened tree resin. Not a true mineral — a mineraloid (no crystals inside).
Superpower: Traps insects, then seals out air and water. See the bugs in this piece of Chiapas amber? A 50-million-year-old snapshot!
Note: This photo is from the web (we didn't catch amber on camera at the show).
📷 Web photo: GiveMeMollusks via Wikimedia Commons (CC0) — not from Rocktoberfest.
📚 Science class link: fossil preservation — how details survive millions of years.

★ Celestite SKY STONE

Named after the sky itself
What: Strontium sulfate — soft, sky-blue crystals (hardness 3–3.5).
Name game: From Latin caelestis = "heavenly." People saw the blue and thought of the sky!
Bonus: Fireworks use strontium to make brilliant red sparks.
Note: This photo is from the web (we didn't catch celestite on camera at the show).
📷 Web photo: James St. John via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0) — not from Rocktoberfest.
📚 Science class link: minerals are often named for a striking property. A name is a clue!

★ Smoky Quartz RADIOACTIVE TAN

Quartz with a suntan from radiation
What: Quartz (SiO₂) with an epidote coating — the label says "Smoky Quartz with Epidote coating, China."
Cool: Natural radiation knocks tiny defects into the crystal — that's the "smoke."
Same family: Amethyst, clear quartz, agate — all quartz, different outfits!
📚 Science class link: one mineral, many colors — color alone can't ID a mineral.

★ Trilobite TIME STAMP

The fossil that tells time
What: Ancient sea animal with a hard shell, cousin of crabs. Ruled the seas ~300 million years — gone before dinosaurs.
Superpower: Index fossil! Find one in a cliff → that rock is Paleozoic (250–540 million years old). No lab needed.
This one: In the display case for $95.
📚 Science class link: fossils as evidence; index fossils date rock layers.

★ Dinosaur Bone $95!

Bone shape, rock material
What: Real dino bone — but the bone stuff is gone. It's stone now.
How: Mineral water filled every tiny hole in the buried bone and hardened. Shape = bone, material = rock.
The label says: "Dinosaur Bone — Jurassic — Utah — 95-"
📚 Science class link: fossil formation step by step — burial → permineralization → discovery.

★ Uraninite INVISIBLE ENERGY

The mineral that glows with energy (for real)
What: A uranium mineral — dark, heavy, history-making. Marie Curie discovered radioactivity studying uranium minerals like it.
Superpower: Gives off invisible energy all by itself, forever. (It's also why some quartz turned smoky!)
Rule: Look, don't touch unknown minerals — and never lick rocks! 😄
Note: This photo is from the web (we didn't catch uraninite on camera at the show).
📷 Web photo: 책읽는달팽 via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0) — not from Rocktoberfest.
📚 Science class link: minerals have surprising properties — magnetism, fluorescence, radioactivity.

🔬 The Mineral ID Lab

How do scientists tell minerals apart? Not just by looks! They run tests — like detectives with a toolkit.

🎨 Color — pretty but tricky! ✨ Luster — how light bounces off 💪 Hardness — the Mohs scale, 1–10 🖊️ Streak — color of its powder 🔷 Crystal shape — cubes? points? 🔨 Cleavage — how it breaks

🕵️ The Great Quartz Trick

Every rock below is the same mineral — quartz (SiO₂), hardness 7, glassy luster. Only the outfits differ!

Herkimer "Diamond" — super-clear quartz from Middleville, NY. (Not a real diamond — liar! 😄)

Amethyst geodes — purple quartz ($33.99–$79.99). Iron + radiation = purple.

Grape agate — bumpy balls of quartz. Looks like grapes!

Clear quartz point — the plain one! (Yunnan, China)

🎩 The trick: if color were enough, this would be 4 different minerals.
It's ONE. That's why scientists test hardness instead!
🤔 THINK: Amethyst is purple, smoky quartz is brown, agate is banded — all quartz. What ONE test proves it? (tap to reveal)
The hardness test! Every quartz scratches glass (hardness 7). Color changes; hardness doesn't. That's the detective's favorite clue.

🪵 Petrification

Buried wood or bone + mineral-rich water = minerals fill every tiny hole and harden. Shape stays, material becomes rock. That's how petrified wood and dino bone formed!

📚 Fossil formation

🟡 Amber trap

Sticky tree resin catches an insect, hardens, and seals out air and water. Result: a 50-million-year-old bug with every wing vein visible.

📚 Fossil preservation

🌊 Rapid burial

Wyoming's Green River lakes had fine mud and almost no oxygen — no scavengers, no decay. Dead fish became perfect fossils, down to the last fin ray.

📚 Why some fossils are amazing

⏱️ Index fossils

A species that lived briefly but spread worldwide = a time stamp. Trilobites = Paleozoic. Find one, date the whole cliff — no lab needed!

📚 Relative dating of rock layers

💩 Trace fossils

Footprints, burrows, nests, and coprolite (fossil poop!). They record what an animal did — what it ate, how fast it ran — not just what it looked like.

📚 Behavior from fossils

🏔️ Fossils as evidence

Sea fossils on a mountaintop? That mountain was once underwater! Fossils in rock layers prove life on Earth has changed over time.
