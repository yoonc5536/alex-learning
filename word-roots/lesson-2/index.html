🧲 1. Meet the Prefixes

A prefix is added to the front of a root — and it changes the meaning of the word. There are hundreds of prefixes! Let’s begin with three of the most common.

PREFIX- + ROOT = NEW WORD
uni- (one) + corn (horn) = unicorn
Now fill in the blank: A unicorn has just a single horn! 🦄

Three examples straight from your book:

dis- + approve

= disapprove — the opposite of approve, or not approve.

re- + fry

= refry — to fry again.

un- + hurt

= unhurt — not hurt.

💡 Tip: Six prefixes that mean NOT

There are at least six prefixes that mean NOT. Each prefix only goes with certain words — unfed can’t be irfed! Most of the time, if it sounds right to you, it’s correct, but check unfamiliar words in the dictionary.

un- as in unfed
im- as in impolite
il- as in illegal
a- as in amoral
in- as in inability
ir- as in irrational

🔚 Lesson 3 — Suffixes

Now that you understand what a prefix is, learning about the other end of the word is easy! When letters are added to the end of a root, they’re called a suffix.

Coming up: act (to do) + -ive (tending to) = active · dent (tooth) + -ist (one who) = dentist · ego (I, self) + -ism (state of being) = egoism
