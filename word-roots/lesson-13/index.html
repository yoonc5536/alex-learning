🚚 1. Meet the Prefixes, Root & Suffixes

This lesson mixes four prefixes, one root, and three suffixes — all on the same team. The star of the show is fer (to carry, bear, produce).

PREFIX + ROOT + SUFFIX = NEW WORD
trans- (across) + fer (to carry) + -able (able to be) = transferable
Able to be carried across from one place to another! 🚚

Three examples straight from your book:

con- + fer

= confer — to talk with another person or persons in order to produce a decision.

trans- + fer

= transfer — to carry across from one place to another.

in- + fer + t-ile

= infertile — relating to not able to produce crops or offspring.

💡 Tip: The tricky prefix in-

The prefix in- has two meanings: in, into and not. In infertile, it means NOT — not able to produce. Use the rest of the word to figure out which meaning the book wants!

in- = not, as in infertile
con- = with, as in confer
trans- = across, as in transfer
