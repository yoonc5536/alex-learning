📦 1. Meet the Prefixes & the Root

A prefix is added to the front of a root — and it changes the meaning. This lesson has six prefixes that all join the same root: pose (to place, to put).

PREFIX- + pose = NEW WORD
com- (together) + pose (to put) = compose
A composer puts together the notes of a song! 🎵

Three word-builds straight from your book’s Activity A:

ex- (out) + pose (to put)

= expose — to put out in the open; to reveal or make known.

pro- (forward) + pose (to put)

= propose — to put forward as an offer for consideration.

dis- (apart) + pose (to put)

= dispose — to place apart; to throw away or get rid of something.

💡 Tip: im- can mean two things!

In this lesson, im- means in, into — as in impose (im- + pose: to place a burden on someone). But you may have also seen im- meaning not, as in impolite (not polite)! The same prefix can have more than one meaning — use the word’s parts and the sentence to figure out which one fits.
