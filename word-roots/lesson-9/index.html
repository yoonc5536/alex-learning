⚖️ 1. Meet the Root & Suffixes

A root is the middle of the word, and a suffix is added to the end of a root — and it changes the meaning of the word.

ROOT + SUFFIX = NEW WORD
medi (half, middle, halfway between) + -ate (to make, to act) = mediate

Four examples straight from your book:

medi + -ate

= mediate — to act or come between opposing sides to solve a problem.

medi + -um

= medium — of a middle size, amount, or quality; neither large nor small.

medi + -an

= median — something that is related to or in the middle.

medi + at + -or

= mediator — one who acts between opposing sides to solve a problem.

📰 Read the full Gazette article

“The weedy median in our small town goes right down the middle of Main Street. Old Mrs. Ringo planted bright flowers and kept up the medium-sized bushes, trees, and ferns. When she was unable to do it anymore, three groups in town each thought they should take over. Our own Chu Ying spoke to the town council about the problem. The council voted to have the garden club two towns over mediate the issue.

Amazingly, the mediator solved the issue quickly. Everyone was pleased, even the flowers—but especially Chu, who is just nine years old!”

💡 Etymology tip: mediocre

The word mediocre is from two roots: medi and ocris (stony mountain). It originally meant going only halfway up the mountain. The use of this word has evolved over time to mean something done half as well as is possible. This is an excellent example of word etymology.
